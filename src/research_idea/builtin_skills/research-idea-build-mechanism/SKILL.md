---
name: research-idea-build-mechanism
description: Build a research idea's core operation and information path during propose; derive technical questions and adapt suitable methods during technical search planning, design_transfer, develop or refine.
---

## Input

Use the supplied candidate, motivation, source evidence, constraints, and any existing method specification. Do not inspect repositories or execute anything; describe missing inspections through the current task's `next_action`.

In plan_search, apply this procedure only when purpose is technical_design. Strategy discovery precedes an idea: return empty design_axes and query axis_ids there, without inventing a technical decomposition.

In propose, use steps 2–4 and 6 to think through the mechanism, then give the reader its decisive operations rather than the full design analysis. The main idea fields explain the target, a workable input-to-output route, why it changes the learner's or decision maker's behavior, and the substantive difference from closest work; usually one short paragraph each. Keep the actual acquisition operation in construction_path, including the additional observable basis for a decisive check and how its outcome changes the next action. Explain why the new source, observation or use of information can reach what the original process missed; asking a model to check again does not itself explain that change. Preserve detailed checks, remaining errors and downstream treatment in information_path for the detailed report. Each paragraph advances the explanation instead of repeating the same distinctions. Return the current PROPOSE schema. The two technical angles, training details and second-phase fields below belong to technical design, not the first-phase explanation.

## Steps

1. Start from the idea and its derived research_need. Identify two complementary technical questions that must be answered for the idea to work. For each, explain why the idea requires it, the function a method must provide, and promising method families to investigate. The angles are functional difficulties, not generic labels such as implementation and evaluation. Choose them before finding techniques; do not retrofit a reason to a favorite module. One technique may address both angles.
2. Follow the central information path from observable inputs to the method's output. Explain what each consequential operation inspects, how it transforms or infers information, what it returns and how the next operation uses that result. Identify when the information exists and the interface it crosses. If acquiring a signal performs the essential inference, include that acquisition in the mechanism and, where appropriate, in the design questions. A named parser, teacher, retrieval system or checker does not explain how it obtains the required meaning or supervision. Put relevant dimensions or shapes in the method text when known; do not invent implementation facts.
3. Look for a reusable principle wherever it fits in computer science, including another subfield. Map the shared structure, original assumptions and observable inputs to this problem. Recover how the source method obtains its decisive signals, not only what it does once those signals exist. Identify the reusable backbone or implementation and the smallest required changes. Explain the resulting technique through the target setting: which bottleneck it addresses, which objects and signals it operates on, which operation changes, and why the change is necessary here. Retain attribution for the borrowed principle and separate source-supported components from proposed components. For every added component, ask what breaks if a simple rule or existing component replaces it.
4. Explain how acquisition quality affects the mechanism. For a central inferred input, identify what evidence or observable outcome checks it, what that check establishes, and which meaningful error it cannot resolve. Traceability, constrained output and agreement can serve useful checks, but their connection to semantic correctness needs its own reasoning. A downstream procedure operating only on a constructed representation cannot independently establish that the representation captured the source correctly. Describe how uncertain or partial inputs still contribute to the intended learning or decision, instead of placing a perfect-information prerequisite before the method. Preserve this reasoning in `idea_core.information_path` when that field is part of the active schema.
5. Make training explicit: trainable and frozen parameters, supervision acquisition, objective, and how the learning signal reaches the changed parameters. For discrete or nondifferentiable operations, specify a justified optimization route or leave that link unverified. For inference-only methods, state why training fields are inapplicable. Reconstruct inference from information available at that time, separating evaluator-only labels and future information. Name major resource dependencies when they affect the claimed benefit; do not invent call counts or detailed budgets.
6. Compare suitable routes while preserving the selected capability and research question. A different information source, representation or objective may solve an acquisition difficulty without giving up the goal. Distinguish a practical starting implementation from the intended setting: explain which part of the mechanism it can support and what methodological work is still needed to reach that setting. Choosing only readily certified inputs does not by itself solve the acquisition problem. Describe what variable changes relative to closest work and what distinct observable consequence follows; retain unresolved comparisons for targeted reading.

## Output
When mapping a source technique to the target, state what plays the role of what: objects, observations, decisions and feedback. Identify how the needed information would be obtained here, which source condition carries over, and which requires a proposed adaptation. Compare against the simplest viable route before adding another component. An objection calls for a reasoned design choice or a specific unresolved technical question, not automatic complexity or a smaller research ambition.


Return only the active task JSON. For design_transfer, give up to three options answering the reading question: source technique → shared structure → conditions → proposed adaptation → reason it helps → simpler alternative. Leave the compatibility field first_test empty. Cite the supplied source evidence for the original technique; describe the transfer as a proposal. Empty options with a concrete reading gap are appropriate when the supplied passages do not explain a usable technique. For develop/refine, suggest one or two suitable routes and record them in design_decisions with first_test empty; borrowed options are not a required module list. Give `method.summary` as a connected explanation of the actual mechanism, including how it obtains the central information and why its main actions work together. State the chosen operation and its purpose directly; replace generic caveats with the concrete input or design choice that matters. Keep the other method fields concise: key operations, usable information and important dependencies; no exhaustive state/action contracts or experimental protocols. Fill `method.base_implementation`, `changes`, `inputs`, `outputs`, `trainable_parameters`, `frozen_parameters`, `objective`, `supervision`, `inference_steps`, `resource_requirements`, and `assumptions`. Coordinate these with `idea_core`, `motivation.why_this_method`, `novelty_claim`, and `feasibility.required_signals`; retain all other required fields. Attach evidence IDs only to supplied source facts. Label design choices and predictions as proposals.

For technical plan_search, save the two questions in design_axes before writing queries. Link each query by axis_ids and cover both angles. For design_transfer, link each usable option to the angle whose required_function it serves. For develop/refine, retain the planned angle IDs in design_axes and link the chosen design_decisions through axis_ids; explain how the choices work together to realize the idea. Search directions may cross CS subfields when their problem structure fits. A borrowed technique needs a target-specific adaptation, not just a new name.

Give each angle a short question a reader can understand without specialist terminology. Explain below it what must be achieved, why the idea needs it, and how a method could help; move technical conditions out of the heading into those sentences. In method.summary and design_decisions, use connected paragraphs to explain actual operations and why they fit together. Unpack necessary terms and acronyms on first use. Keep internal implementation fields brief, but give the public explanation enough space to be understood without consulting the paper. Preserve the scientific meaning while making the language easier to read.

## Failure behavior

An unspecified interface becomes a concrete access condition. For a missing signal, work on the acquisition operation or choose another plausible route within the intended setting, updating the actual method and signal list together. If the change alters the scientific hypothesis, explain the changed rationale and prediction. Keep the important target visible when a route remains incomplete, and name the technical question needed to advance it. Do not fabricate code availability, measurements, or completed experiments.
