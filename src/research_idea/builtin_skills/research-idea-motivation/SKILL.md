## Input

Use the supplied topic, constraints, evidence, candidate, and current task schema. Treat this skill as a reasoning procedure, never scientific evidence. Do not access tools, scripts, files, or services; propose needed actions to the controller.

Aim for a coherent idea worth developing toward leading CCF-A research: a consequential problem, a non-obvious insight, a core intervention that follows from it, and a substantive contribution after accounting for the closest work. Explain what the community would learn or be able to do differently. Judge this complete argument rather than rewarding a broad topic, a complicated method or individually plausible components. Strengthen weak links within the current task; do not introduce scores, approval labels or demands for completed experiments.

## Steps

1. Start with a concrete background setting and what it needs to accomplish. Reconstruct what the closest papers actually establish, under which conditions, and what assumptions their answers share. Explain how those details lead to a consequential unresolved question; an omitted comparison or untried combination is not enough. Separate source-backed observations from author interpretations and your inferred explanation. Attach existing evidence IDs to supported facts.
2. Establish the problem before its explanation: who needs to achieve what, which difficulty remains, and what important capability or understanding it affects. Explain why the conditions arise from the intended setting or a meaningful scientific question. Separate the source-supported observation, an author's stated risk and your causal hypothesis; a limitation statement alone does not establish a frequent or measured failure. The problem may be a reasoned new question, but its importance must survive a wrong causal guess as well as a failed technique. Do not output worked examples or invent restrictions to make a preferred method necessary.
3. Reconstruct the strongest available alternative as a complete response. Include the information it can actively obtain, the tools it can use, and the updates it can make. Explain which part it addresses and the concrete difficulty it still leaves; an omitted comparison or setup name does not establish that gap. Similar titles trigger comparison, not abandonment; unknown full-text details remain unknown.
   Use the claim audit now: author claim → tested conditions and controls → reported result → warranted conclusion. Do not let a broad abstract claim erase a concrete open problem. An already proposed mechanism remains prior art even if its claimed efficacy was not adequately established; the new contribution must then be a meaningful question, explanation, boundary or justified change.
   Separate motivation novelty from method novelty. The main overlap question is whether the same observed difficulty, causal rationale and intended scientific claim are already addressed. Reusing an existing method is acceptable. Reframe the story around a different evidence-backed problem, explanation or boundary when justified; state the change and a discriminating prediction rather than merely changing terminology.
4. Derive the motivation before choosing an intervention. Explain a plausible cause of the independently motivated problem, and what remains unresolved by the strongest current response under the actual conditions. Then state the research need: what must be understood or made possible to overcome this limitation? Identify who would change the proposed variable and what they can actually control. Importance alone does not supply this inference, and naming a preferred technique is not a motivation. The idea must meet this need through a clear causal principle. A proposed cause remains a hypothesis; do not turn a missing experiment into proof that the existing approach cannot work.
5. Explain expected progress beyond an arbitrary metric delta or the generic observation that clearer information and more resources can help. Identify the non-obvious relation, new capability or understanding, or the binding limitation removed. Each link must justify the next: a compelling background alone does not justify a weak problem, and a sophisticated technique does not justify an unrelated intervention. Efficiency can be substantive when it removes a binding constraint; a new explanation or failure boundary can matter without improving accuracy. Neither an added module nor a renamed tradeoff establishes progress alone.
6. Challenge the motivation with the strongest plausible counterargument: misread evidence, a low-value failure, an existing simple solution, or an intervention unrelated to the cause. Resolve the specific objection before polishing the story. Do not replace this reasoning with an overall score.
7. Identify the simplest alternative and reason about whether it already answers the same meaningful question. During refinement keep the background, problem, hypothesis, method and prediction consistent; explain substantive reframing.
8. Preserve the substantive research question. Do not narrow it to a small invented task merely because that task is easy to implement. If a simple response already solves the problem, improve the research insight or choose a stronger angle; do not rescue the same weak idea by adding artificial costs, restrictions or protocol detail. In map, retain useful distinct directions in opportunities with their literature derivation, core insight, contribution and plausible information path. In propose, choose the strongest substantive direction and explain the choice; opportunity_id preserves that link. A crowded field can contain an important unanswered question.

## Output
When choosing a question, consider a contradictory result, an untested assumption, a transferable mechanism, a new operating regime or an informative diagnosis as appropriate. Use these as alternative ways to understand the literature, not a quota of ideas or extra roles. Prefer a consequential question with one clear mechanism. State what would be learned if the strongest simple alternative explains the effect; useful negative evidence can change the explanation or design.


Return only the current task JSON. In propose, fill each candidate's `motivation`; in develop/refine, fill top-level `motivation`. Write it as a connected argument: `background` states the setting and need; `existing_work` explains the strongest relevant response and what it achieves; `problem_observation` and `importance` identify the residual difficulty and consequence; `causal_explanation` explains why it may arise; `existing_approach_limit` connects that cause to the limits of the current response; `research_need` derives the motivation; `why_this_method` states the idea that meets it; `expected_progress` explains the new capability or understanding. Each part should make the next necessary, not repeat a generic claim about importance. Retain `basis`, `evidence_ids`, `simplest_alternative` and `necessity_test` for source distinction and internal reasoning. Use `source_observation` for an observed problem with source IDs; use `research_hypothesis` for a plausible phenomenon or question to investigate. Hypothetical premises do not need invented citations. In review, identify the specific weak inference in this chain through `motivation_assessment`, `issues`, and `revision_instructions`. Keep all other required task fields.

The first part explains the idea: what is the new substantive problem, why does it matter, and how does this idea address it? State the setting, bottleneck, consequence and core intervention in concrete language. Keep `why_this_method` at the level of that intervention's purpose and causal principle. The second part supplies optional technical references and the adaptations they suggest; it need not repeat those three answers. Avoid generic '尚未确认' or originality disclaimers. A meaningful condition belongs in the relevant input or technical rationale. No illustrative cases or experimental plans are requested. Keep source facts and proposed hypotheses distinct without repeating cautionary labels.

Write for a reader who knows what LLMs and agents are but is unfamiliar with the topic's specialist concepts. Explain the setting and each causal step in ordinary language, with enough complete sentences to follow the argument. Name what the agent, environment or method actually does. Introduce a technical term only after explaining its meaning; keep terminology consistent. Each field continues the same argument rather than compressing it into a label or repeating a slogan. Preserve source scope and proposed hypotheses when simplifying the language. More explanation should clarify the reasoning, without adding invented cases or experimental plans.

## Failure behavior

If importance or causality lacks evidence, mark the uncertainty in the applicable text or review enum and identify the decisive missing evidence. Do not substitute confidence scores, invented experiments, or novelty claims. A weak current rationale warrants a concrete revision, not automatic termination. State the proposed explanation directly and identify a distinguishing consequence; avoid generic disclaimers about whether the idea is globally original or experiments have run.

Deliver a concrete proposal and improve it constructively. Normal idea-stage assumptions belong in concise internal feasibility reasoning. Offer a motivation-led reframing when the starting story is weak. During refinement answer each critique explicitly: adopt, adapt, decline with reasons, or test. Preserve the user's direction; the initial hypothesis is allowed to improve, with the change explained.

## Synthetic decision contrast

“Add a confidence head to save tokens” leaves the motivation unresolved. A sharper hypothesis asks whether observable signals predict benefit from additional computation, then compares against fixed-budget allocation. This is an illustrative test proposal, not a demonstrated finding or novel idea.
