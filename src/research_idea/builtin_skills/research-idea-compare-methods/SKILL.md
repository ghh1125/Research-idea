# Compare mechanisms and conditions

## Inputs
Use the candidate problem, hypothesis, method specification, source records and verified evidence IDs. Similar titles identify reading targets; they do not establish duplication.

## Procedure
1. Restate the candidate's smallest central claim. Separate inherited components from its proposed new interaction, explanatory claim, boundary, or capability.
   Start with motivation overlap: compare the observed problem, the limitation that makes it important, the causal explanation and the knowledge the study would establish. Shared methods are reusable tools, not an automatic duplicate verdict. A new motivation can justify an existing method when it answers a materially different question, distinguishes explanations, or establishes an untested boundary. A shared broad topic is not a shared motivation. Express these comparisons within problem, mechanism and claim, with source evidence.
2. During first-round proposal, fill positioning for the closest relevant sources: addressed_problem, remaining_question and contribution with that paper’s evidence. Improve a motivation already answered by supplied work before proposing it. During develop/refine, fill the six detailed comparisons: problem, mechanism, information, objective, evaluation, claim. Give concrete prior_detail and proposed_detail, relation same/different/unknown, and evidence_ids from that paper.
3. Trace both actual information paths: what is available during training, inference, and evaluation; which objects and relationships are used; how they are obtained or checked; what intervention changes the computation. Separate assumptions used to analyze a method from inputs or conditions required to run it. An idealized decomposition in a proof is not automatically an annotation or oracle needed by the algorithm. Explain what the proposed intervention actually requires the learner or decision maker to do differently and which capability should follow; reorganizing the presentation alone does not establish that change. Compare conditions rather than technique names.
4. Ask whether the prior method already implies the candidate's central prediction under comparable conditions. Distinguish topical overlap, shared problem, shared mechanism, and actual core-claim coverage.
   Judge the whole contribution: existing components can reveal a new interaction or finding. Finding the components in separate papers does not show that one work already answered the combined question. A renamed setting adds little if directly using the existing solution already gives the claimed answer. State the specific covered claim and its paper, then explain what the remaining difference would teach or enable. Paper counts and lexical similarity are not scientific overlap judgments.
5. Compare with the strongest normal use of the closest supplied methods: include their ordinary adaptation, information access, exploration and updating. Explain whether directly using them for this problem already produces the proposed effect. If it does, identify the distinct question or finding that remains; do not rename that use as a new mechanism. If it does not, identify the specific missing requirement and how the idea changes it. Never make the comparison favorable by withholding information or capabilities those methods normally have.
   Challenge a difference at the level on which novelty is claimed: would it survive renaming modules or giving the prior method comparable data, resources and access? A dataset swap or unmotivated tradeoff alone is not a substantive gap. For a claimed new explanation, name the particular relationship the explanation establishes and why the prior findings do not already imply it; the phrase "explain the underlying principle" is not that relationship.
6. When related work leaves a consequential boundary or assumption open, retain that route and explain what new consequence or understanding it offers. If it truly covers the claim, record the narrow overlap and seek a meaningful reformulation without silently changing the hypothesis.
   Before abandoning a candidate, give a motivation-led reframing route when the evidence supports one. The story may change; explain the changed problem and the resulting scientific difference. Do not demand a new algorithm for an explanatory or boundary contribution, and do not label inherited machinery as a new mechanism.

## Output
In propose, fill positioning and idea_core.essential_difference. Give the reader a concrete distinction: what the closest method already enables in normal use, what important question or ability remains, and what our idea adds to address it. In develop/refine, keep essential_difference consistent with the chosen route, prior_art.overlaps and novelty_claim in the active method schema. Keep the comparison at the level of the mechanism, consequence and contribution; do not add worked examples or experiment plans. Supply follow-up queries only for a consequential unresolved relation. Technical borrowing from another domain is useful even when the mechanism itself is established; distinguish its source problem from the question our idea answers. A justified local difference is not proof of global novelty.

## Missing evidence
Use unknown for unavailable details and uncertain for unsupported coverage. A covered recommendation needs a same-claim, six-dimensional comparison backed by full-text evidence, including reported_result evidence in the evaluation comparison; its semantics still require review. An author claim alone cannot establish that the problem was empirically solved. Unproven efficacy does not make a preexisting mechanism novel. Never reject an idea merely because a related title exists.
